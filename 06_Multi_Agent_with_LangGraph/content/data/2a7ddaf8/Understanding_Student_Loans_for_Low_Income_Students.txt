Many low-income students face multiple barriers, including lack of financial support, lower income prospects post-graduation, and higher rates of default. According to the latest Federal Reserve reports, households with student debt are often at a higher risk of facing financial hardship, reflecting a cycle of economic strain that can hinder their overall well-being.

The recent implementation of income-driven repayment plans, such as the SAVE plan introduced in August 2023, aims to alleviate some of these pressures. This initiative proposes to reduce monthly payments based on income, which could significantly benefit low-income borrowers, potentially allowing for loan forgiveness after a period of consistent payments. 

Yet, despite these efforts, many borrowers remain at risk. For instance, financial stability is particularly challenging for students of color and first-generation college-goers, who often lack appropriate resources or generational wealth.
